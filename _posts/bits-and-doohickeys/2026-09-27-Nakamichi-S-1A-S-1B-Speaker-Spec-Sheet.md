## Identity

The S-1A and S-1B are the left and right speakers of Nakamichi's RS-1 "Auxiliary Music System," a compact system dated 1988. No official Nakamichi spec sheet for the speakers has been found online, so this sheet combines forum-quoted service-manual details, seller listings, and a hands-on teardown of this pair.

## Drivers

A 2-way design: a roughly 4.5" paper-cone woofer rated 8 Ω / 15 W and a 2" cone tweeter rated 8 Ω / 6 W, both generic OEM units with stamped steel frames. Ratings and part numbers below are read from the magnets of this pair; sizes combine the service manual quoted on [Tapeheads](https://www.tapeheads.net/threads/any-info-specs-manuals-for-nakamichi-cp1-r1-sp1-mini-system.36007/) with inspection.

| Spec | Woofer | Tweeter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type | Paper cone, zinc-plated stamped steel frame | Small cone with center cap (not a dome), stamped steel frame |
| Nominal size | About 4.5" frame (service manual: 5"; listings: 4") | 2" (service manual); measured: 52 mm round frame, 74 mm overall with ears, 48 mm baffle cutout |
| Impedance (stamped) | 8 Ω | 8 Ω |
| Power rating (stamped) | 15 W | 6 W |
| Part number (stamped, partly legible) | Begins "4002…" | 4001286138D |

| Connections | Push-on spade terminals | Push-on spade terminals |
| Wiring | Red and black leads | White (through the crossover capacitor) and black leads |

The tweeter has a 52 mm round frame with two slotted ears and sits in a 48 mm hole in the baffle. The slots are 65.06 mm apart, so a replacement needs the same mounting pattern or an adapter.

## Crossover

The entire crossover is one 3.3 µF / 50 V bipolar electrolytic capacitor in series with the tweeter; the woofer runs unfiltered. This was confirmed by inspection of this pair.

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Topology | First-order (6 dB/octave) high-pass on the tweeter only |
| Component | Bipolar (non-polarized) electrolytic, marked "3.3 MFD 50 WV" (MFD = microfarads) |
| Location | Soldered to the back of the rear terminal plate; the white tweeter lead is crimped to it |
| Nominal crossover point | About 6 kHz, assuming a flat 8 Ω tweeter load |
| Woofer filter | None; the woofer rolls off naturally |
| Level matching (L-pad) | None |
| Impedance compensation (Zobel) | None |

The real crossover point differs because a tweeter's impedance isn't a flat 8 Ω. A like-for-like restoration part is a 3.3 µF polypropylene film capacitor rated 100 V or more. The capacitors are branded Luxon. Measured on this pair with a V79 multimeter, with the tweeter lead disconnected: S-1A about 4.3 µF and S-1B about 4.43 µF, both roughly 30% above the marked 3.3 µF and matched to each other within about 3%. At 4.4 µF the nominal crossover point is about 4.5 kHz, assuming a flat 8 Ω tweeter load.

## Reception

No magazine reviews from the period have been found. Modern owner opinions are split, and condition after 35 years seems to explain much of the difference.

- **Positive:** A 2018 [HiFi Engine](https://www.hifiengine.com/manual_library/nakamichi/rs-1.shtml) reviewer rated the system very good for its size, with good mids and treble and surprisingly good bass. A 2013 [Audiokarma](https://audiokarma.org/forums/threads/nakamichi-r1-mini-receiver-wow.301788/) owner who bought the system new about 25 years earlier never wanted to upgrade. A 2016 owner in the same thread found the speakers pretty good given their size and small amp.
- **Negative:** In the same Audiokarma thread, one 2010 member called the stock speakers poor, and another heard them distort at higher volume but suspected his pair had been abused. A 2018 member called them poor too, though his receiver may have needed new capacitors.
- **Context:** A 2006 [Retro Thing](https://www.retrothing.com/2006/08/nakamichi_syste.html) post noted that compact systems like this were once a sign of sophistication and expense.

## This pair

Bought on eBay; both speakers work, sound clear, and remain completely stock. Notes from the teardown:

- Woofer surrounds intact, with no foam rot.
- Minor cosmetic damage to the outer shell.
- Some baffle screws have surface rust, suggesting past humidity. Check cabinet edges for swelling; reuse screws after wire-brushing and tighten gently into the particleboard.
- Tweeter foam gasket is doubled; woofer has no gasket.
- Damping felt covers only part of the rear wall.
- Original Luxon 3.3 µF / 50 V bipolar capacitors in place; both measure about 4.3-4.4 µF (see Crossover). One tweeter was damaged during teardown: a dented center dust cap and crushed paper along the outer cone edge. Replacement is under consideration.
- Current setup: onboard Realtek audio or a UGREEN USB DAC into a Nobsound NS-06G amp. Swapping the DAC made no audible difference.

## Upgrade notes

- **Restoration:** Replace the original capacitor with a 3.3 µF polypropylene film capacitor rated 100 V or more; bag the original.
- **Full modern rebuild:** The Overnight Sensations kit uses a HiVi B4N 4" woofer and Dayton ND20FA-6 tweeter in a 4.5 L box tuned to 53 Hz with a 1-3/8" x 6" port ([SoundImports](https://www.soundimports.eu/en/overnight-audio-component.html)). Its 9" x 6" x 8-3/8" cabinet ([Parts Express](https://projectgallery.parts-express.com/speaker-projects/better-boom-box/)) is nearly the same size as the S-1. Building it on a new baffle and terminal plate would keep the original parts intact and swappable. Expect more bass than stock.
